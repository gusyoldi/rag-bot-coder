# Jobs to be Done (JTBD)

Jobs to be Done is a framing for understanding why customers "hire" a product:
they are trying to make progress in a specific circumstance, not buy features.

## Core idea

A **job** is the progress a person wants in a situation. It has functional,
emotional, and social dimensions. Competing solutions include other products,
workarounds, and doing nothing.

Example: a user does not "want a calendar app"; they want to reliably show up
prepared to meetings without anxiety (functional + emotional job).

## How a Product Owner uses JTBD

1. Interview or observe users about recent struggles and workarounds.
2. Write job statements: *When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome].*
3. Map current solutions and where they fail (forces of progress: push, pull,
   anxiety, habit).
4. Prioritize opportunities that help users complete important jobs better,
   not ideas that only sound innovative internally.
5. Translate jobs into outcomes and then into backlog items (stories, experiments).

## Useful questions

- What were you trying to get done when you used this?
- What did you try before?
- What almost stopped you from switching?
- What does "done" look like in that moment?

## Pitfalls

- Confusing a job with a persona stereotype.
- Jumping from a job statement straight to a UI without validating the outcome.
- Treating JTBD as a one-off workshop instead of an ongoing discovery lens.
